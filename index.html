Southern Colorado Healthcare
Needs Assessment

Analyzing the healthcare landscape, insurance disparities, and systemic hurdles across our communities.

Based on ~1,600 community survey responses

Executive Summary

Based on individual survey responses from roughly 1,600 community members across Southern Colorado, the healthcare landscape is heavily shaped by an aging demographic, widespread struggles with dental care, and surprising disparities in how well different insurance types protect patients from financial burdens.

Total Responses by Region

  • San Luis Valley (811): 923 respondents
  • Lower Arkansas Valley (810): 384 respondents
  • Upper Arkansas Valley (812): 219 respondents
  • *Additional 30 respondents fell outside these specific target regions.
Part 1

Overview of Results

Examining the insurance landscape, the reality of cost barriers, and the universal systemic hurdles affecting Southern Colorado.

The Insurance Landscape

The community is predominantly covered by public insurance. Medicare is the dominant provider, covering nearly half of all respondents (760 individuals), which strongly suggests an aging population with high chronic healthcare needs.

Medicaid is the second most common (344 individuals), followed by Employer-Sponsored coverage (306 individuals). Only a small fraction of the population reported being entirely Uninsured (75 individuals).

The Reality of Cost Barriers

When evaluating who actually delays or skips care due to cost, the data reveals critical gaps in the modern healthcare safety net:

The Uninsured

Unsurprisingly, 61.3% of uninsured individuals faced prohibitive cost barriers in the past 12 months, making them the most vulnerable group.

Employer-Sponsored Paradox

Shockingly, individuals with Employer-Sponsored insurance reported the second-highest rate of cost barriers at 38.6%. Working-class families are struggling with high deductibles and severe out-of-pocket maximums.

Medicaid's Protective Effect

Medicaid recipients reported the lowest rate of cost barriers (26.7%). Its structure of zero or low co-pays is highly effective, outperforming Medicare (30.7%) and Commercial plans (29.9%).

Universal Systemic Hurdles

Beyond insurance, the physical healthcare infrastructure is struggling to meet demand.

Dental Care is in Crisis

Across all demographics, affording dental care and securing a timely dental appointment are the most universally reported barriers to health in the dataset.

Navigation and Wait Times

Almost 1 in 5 respondents reported that simply "finding available needed services" or dealing with extended "wait times" prevented them from getting care. The infrastructure is present, but it is bottlenecked.

Part 2

Regional Disparities Analysis

While the overall data paints a picture of systemic challenges, separating the data into the three primary geographic zones reveals that each community is fighting a distinctly different battle.

Comparison of systemic barriers (Wait times, Navigation, Transportation) across the three primary regions.

San Luis Valley (Zip codes 811)

The Battle: Logistical Gridlock and Primary Care Shortages

The San Luis Valley experiences the highest rates of friction when it comes to time, access, and primary care logistics.

19.2%
Highest Wait Times

Cited wait times as a direct barrier to care, the highest of all regions.

20.5%
Navigation Struggles

Reported an inability to find available needed services.

17.9%
Primary Care Deficit

Unable to secure timely primary care with a General Doctor.

Conclusion: The SLV doesn't necessarily suffer from the worst financial barriers; rather, it suffers from a severe provider shortage and administrative gridlock that prevents patients from accessing the care they can afford.

Upper Arkansas Valley (Zip codes 812)

The Battle: The Heavy Burden of Cost

While the UAV generally reports lower wait times and fewer issues getting a primary doctor appointment, its residents are drowning in out-of-pocket costs. They reported the highest cost barriers across nearly every medical category.

  • 22.8% skipped dental care due to cost.
  • 18.3% could not afford a specialist (compared to ~11% in the other valleys).
  • 15.1% skipped medications due to cost.
  • 13.7% could not afford to see their primary care provider.
Conclusion: The UAV has adequate physical access to care, but the local economy or the specific design of local insurance plans leaves residents highly exposed to medical debt and out-of-pocket expenses.

Lower Arkansas Valley (Zip codes 810)

The Battle: Geographic and Transportation Hurdles

The Lower Arkansas Valley generally reported the lowest rates of cost barriers and the fewest issues with appointment availability. However, their primary obstacle is geographic isolation.

Transportation as a Barrier

Over 13.3% of respondents in the LAV cited transportation as a direct barrier to receiving medical care. This is significantly higher than the San Luis Valley (9.0%) and the Upper Arkansas Valley (8.2%).

Conclusion: For the LAV, the barrier is not necessarily the cost of the doctor or the wait time at the clinic—it is the literal ability to travel from their home to the healthcare facility. Public transit solutions, mobile clinics, or telehealth expansions would yield the highest return on investment in this specific region.
